How it works

The generator inverts the traditional process. Instead of inventing hopefuls and testing them afterward, it declares the constraints first — class stem, route, indication, tone — then composes hundreds of law-abiding candidates and keeps only the ones born farthest from every existing name. Tone is sound symbolism put to work: plosives and closed syllables read as clinical authority; liquids, nasals, and open vowels read as gentle. Every candidate then runs the same three screening passes, entirely in your browser — nothing you type leaves this page.

The passes: first, the written rules — syllable budgets, the INN aversion to letters that travel badly (H, J, K, W, Y), embedded pharmacological stems, digits, and words that promise outcomes or smuggle in routes and anatomy. Second, a POCA-flavored similarity screen — orthographic and phonetic distance combined, in the spirit of the FDA's own POCA program, which the agency distributes free — against every name in the corpus. Third, a density reading: how many existing names sit within a 45% similarity radius. A good name is not merely unclaimed; it is isolated. The orange dot in the mark is a name with density zero — that is the entire doctrine, drawn.

Verdicts come in three shapes. A collision fails. A caution passes with its flags shown. A clean screen is exactly that — a strong contender for the human process that follows, never a substitute for it.

Coverage and limits

Screened against

  • Demonstration corpus: ~300 widely known US generic and brand names
  • Representative subset of USAN/INN pharmacological stems (37 of ~600)
  • Rule set: INN letter guidance, syllable budgets, FDA proprietary-name best practices

Not screened against

  • The full Drugs@FDA, WHO INN, or EMA registries
  • 150+ national medicine registries
  • USPTO, WIPO, or any trademark database
  • Unregistered common-law marks
  • Private pipelines and pending applications
  • Meaning in other languages

About

Brimova is not a medicine. It was the first clean name this screening engine ever produced — coined into the sparsest region its own metric could find — and so the tool's firstborn became the tool's name. In Rome, the nomenclator was the servant who stood at his master's elbow and whispered the names of approaching citizens, so that no one was ever misaddressed. This is that servant, rebuilt in JavaScript. Look-alike, sound-alike drug names injure over a million people a year; the professionals who screen against that are expensive, and most of the world's naming happens without them. This page is for everyone else.

The code is open source and runs entirely client-side. Found a collision this screen should have caught? That is the most valuable mail this project can receive: collisions@brimova.org. Security reports: security@brimova.org. Everything else: info@brimova.org.
